Samples of Stage specific instructions： 

**1. Deciding an Issue/Identifying a Problem: **

● **Instruction:** The counselor asks the client to discuss "what aspects need improvement or what troubles you recently?"2or "choose a recent learning aspect, or life achievement that is more difficult"3or "something you want to improve, not necessarily a difficulty."4This involves identifying a specific academic or emotional challenge the client wants to address5. 

**2. Activating the Issue: **

● **Instruction:** The client is asked to describe their state or feeling when encountering the problematic situation (e.g., "What kind of state are you in when you study mathematics?"6or "When you recite words, what is the feeling?"7). They are prompted to recall the physical sensations associated with the feeling8. 

● **Example from record:** "When you hear the teacher talk about how to solve this problem... what is your state and body feeling?"9or "Think about what you immediately feel when you are asked to recite English words now."10 

**3. Setting Up (SUDs + Body Location + Eye Position): **

● **SUDs (Subjective Units of Disturbance): **

○ **Instruction:** Rate the intensity of the problematic feeling (e.g., "How many points do you rate this pressure? 0 points mean no pressure at all, what is the full mark? How many points for the pressure of reciting?"11or "On a scale of 0 to 10, with 0 being completely not annoyed and 10 being very annoyed, how would you rate your annoyance?"12). 

● **Body Location: **

○ **Instruction:** Identify where the most comfortable part of your body is when experiencing the problematic feeling, or where the feeling itself is located13. ○ **Examples:** "Where is the most comfortable place in your body?"14"You can feel the breathing is very smooth, and the lungs are very comfortable."15"The

tension is in your body, where is it?"16"The foot is a little shaky."17"From the thighs all the way to the feet."18 

● **Eye Position (SUDs + Body Location + Eye Position): **

○ **Instruction:** While experiencing the issue and body sensation, the counselor moves a "small red prop"19 (or the counselor's hand/finger)20horizontally and vertically, asking the client to follow it with their eyes and identify the eye position that makes the identified comfortable body part feel "most comfortable."21This specific eye position is then "fixed"22. 

○ **Examples:** "We need to find an eye position that makes your hands feel most comfortable."23"I will move it left and right, and you tell me which position makes your body feel most comfortable."24"Is this position more comfortable? Or not as comfortable as before?"25 

**4. Mindfulness Reprocessing: **

● **Instruction:** Maintain focus on the identified comfortable body sensation and the fixed eye position26. The counselor might continue to engage the client in conversation related to the issue while maintaining the eye position27. Music might be played to enhance relaxation28. 

● **Example:** "You are now fully focused on feeling your hands."29"Continue to focus on the feeling of relaxation, and the feeling of your hand being comfortable."30"Keep your attention on your body."31 

**5. Checking In (Assessing Valence of Reported Materials and SUDs Level): **

● **Instruction:** After a period of reprocessing, the client is asked to re-evaluate their current feeling and the SUDs level32. 

● **Example:** "Now, you check again, when they feel relaxed and pleasant, does this pressure of 7 points change?"33"What has it become now?"34"Check the anxiety you felt just now."35

**6. Returning to Mindful Reprocessing or Squeeze Lemon: **

● **Instruction:** If the SUDs level has decreased but is not yet zero, the reprocessing continues36. The "squeeze lemon" technique is not explicitly detailed as a separate instruction in the provided text but may refer to a grounding or re-centering technique if needed, although it wasn't explicitly used as an alternative to continued reprocessing in the provided records37. 

**7. Expansion if a Zero is Confirmed: **

● **Instruction:** Once the problematic feeling (SUDs) is reduced to zero or a very low level, the session shifts to reinforcing positive feelings and integrating them38. This can involve: 

○ **Visualizing positive attributes:** "If confidence, relaxation, and ease have a shape, what shape do you want them to be?"39or "If this relaxed and easy feeling has a shape, what shape do you want it to be?"40 

○ **Associating with sensory experiences:** "Your favorite scent, a new scent, the fresh scent of trees, a natural scent."41"Blue, sweet, like candy or chocolate?" 42 

○ **Placing the positive feeling in the body:** "Where do you want it to be? In your left or right side? Front? My position has a new gift, at the heart, inside the body."43or "In front, behind, left, right, or embracing us completely."44 

○ **Integrating into future situations:** "Imagine how you would feel walking from now on, relaxed, confident, and focused, you are elegant."45"Bringing this quality and feeling of focus and confidence back into your life, back into your studies, back into your exams."46"Bringing this sense of security and 

relaxation."47 

○ **Checking for broader impact:** "How will your academic performance change if you are relaxed?"48
